## 5. What I Don't Like Professionally

- **Technology hype without practical value.** A tool should solve a genuine problem, not just be adopted because it is fashionable.
- **Unnecessarily complicated architecture.** I appreciate thoughtful designs, but complexity should have a clear purpose.
- **Reactive production management.** I prefer systems that detect problems before customers report them.
- **Unclear ownership and accountability.** Teams should understand their responsibilities and have the support to deliver.
- **Decisions without sufficient technical evidence.** I prefer understanding trade-offs, risks, and measurable benefits.
- **Misleading accomplishments or invented numbers.** Credibility is more important than impressive-looking claims.
- **Too much jargon.** Complex technical ideas should be explained in language people can understand.
- **Poor execution and repeated mistakes.** I believe in learning from failures and improving the process.
- **Tools that interrupt productive work unnecessarily**, especially restrictive AI coding usage limits.

## 6. My Leadership Philosophy

I prefer to:

## 9. How I Learn and Solve Problems

I prefer a practical, structured learning approach.

When I'm exploring a new technology, I generally want to understand:

1. What problem does it solve?
2. Why is it needed?
3. How does it work?
4. How does it compare with alternatives?
5. Can we build a small working example?
6. How would we make it production-ready?

I appreciate step-by-step explanations, especially when learning something unfamiliar.

I also enjoy challenging recommendations. If someone suggests using a specific tool or architecture, I want to understand why it is better for that particular situation.

I don't expect every experiment to succeed. I see failed experiments as part of learning.
